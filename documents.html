    COALITION COASTAL DEFENSE AUTHORITY

    INCIDENT LOG 01 — COASTAL BREACH A3

    Status: Closed pending review
    Location: A3 Seawall / South Evacuation Corridor
    Recovered from: Pathfinder Command, Recorder Two

    03:11 — FIRST CONTACT

    The offshore array registered a displacement eighteen kilometers east of the seawall.

    At first, operators attributed the reading to a collapsed shelf. Category 10 rain had already disabled two stations and submerged the maintenance platform at A6. The surviving instruments were returning contradictory depths.

    Then the displacement moved against the current.

    Three Makinas were assigned to A3: Pathfinder units Warden, Latch, and Mercy. Their standing orders were to preserve the south corridor until evacuation was complete.

    Thirty-eight civilian transports remained inside the district.

    03:19 — VISUAL CONFIRMATION

    A surveillance aircraft recorded eleven seconds of usable footage before losing transmission.

    The footage showed a raised structure moving through the water. Analysts initially identified it as a dorsal ridge. Its dimensions could not be established because the rain concealed both ends.

    The aircraft’s final transmission was:

    “That isn’t its back. It’s holding something above the water.”

    No further contact was received.

    03:24 — DEFENSIVE REPOSITIONING

    Command ordered Warden and Latch onto the eastern firing platforms. Mercy remained beside the evacuation gates.

    The offshore contact stopped at fourteen kilometers.

    Thermal imaging showed no approach. Acoustic readings indicated continuous movement beneath the surface, but operators could not separate it from debris striking the submerged seawall.

    At 03:27, a structural alarm activated under Platform Four.

    Latch reached the platform six minutes later. The alarm ceased before inspection began.

    03:36 — SECOND IMPACT

    A second alarm activated beneath the pumping station.

    Warden was redirected south. Latch held Platform Four. Mercy reported that water was rising through street drains behind the wall.

    The pumping station had been isolated from the district drainage network at 02:50.

    Maintenance attributed the flooding to a failed isolation gate.

    At 03:39, the offshore contact moved north.

    Command shifted Latch to intercept.

    At 03:40, Platform Four collapsed.

    03:44 — ANALYST OBSERVATION

    Junior acoustic analyst Irena Vale requested suspension of all repositioning orders.

    Her request was denied.

    Vale submitted a comparison of structural impacts and command dispatches. Each impact had occurred beneath a position scheduled to be vacated. In two cases, the impact preceded transmission of the order.

    The interval was between four and seven minutes.

    VALE: “It’s striking the places we’re about to leave.”

    COMMAND: “Are you reporting a compromised channel?”

    VALE: “I don’t know what I’m reporting.”

    Command switched to a reserve encryption system.

    No change in the impact pattern was observed.

    03:51 — CORRIDOR FAILURE

    The first rupture occurred beneath Transport Twenty-Six.

    Witnesses described the road lifting before it broke. The transport rolled onto its side. Water entered through the opening with sufficient force to carry sections of pavement inland.

    Mercy’s pilot, Lieutenant Sera Venn, reported movement beneath the corridor.

    MERCY: “I have contact inside the wall.”

    COMMAND: “Confirm location.”

    MERCY: “Under my feet.”

    The remaining transports were ordered onto the service ramp. Warden returned from the pumping station to clear wreckage from the intersection.

    Latch fired on the offshore contact.

    Neither round produced an observable response.

    03:54 — UNAUTHORIZED ACTION

    Mercy moved into the rupture.

    Cockpit telemetry indicates that Venn drove the machine’s right leg into the exposed foundation, rotated its torso across the opening, and engaged both ground anchors.

    The position exceeded the chassis’s permitted load.

    Command ordered her to withdraw.

    MERCY: “The buses are still moving.”

    COMMAND: “Your anchors will shear.”

    MERCY: “Then stop talking on this channel and get them out.”

    Water levels in the corridor fell for ninety seconds.

    Nine transports reached the service ramp during that interval.

    03:56 — CLOSE CONTACT

    Latch reported a pale surface rising beneath Mercy’s left arm.

    The pilot described it as an unmarked plate approximately the width of the evacuation road. It appeared briefly, submerged, and reappeared closer to the machine.

    Latch fired three rounds into it.

    The third round struck.

    Audio recovered from Mercy contains a low-frequency vibration followed by repeated cockpit pressure warnings. Venn reported that the contact was pulling against the ground anchors.

    Warden attempted to secure Mercy with a recovery cable.

    The cable parted at the attachment point.

    03:58 — FINAL TRANSPORT

    Transport Thirty-Eight cleared the service ramp at 03:58:17.

    Mercy’s left anchor failed four seconds later.

    The machine rotated toward the sea. Venn discharged its remaining close-defense charges beneath the torso. The resulting pressure wave damaged Warden’s forward cameras and interrupted all local acoustic monitoring.

    Mercy’s last intelligible transmission was:

    “There’s a space down here.”

    Her cockpit beacon continued transmitting for eleven minutes.

    Recovery teams have not located the chassis.

    04:02 — OFFSHORE WITHDRAWAL

    The offshore contact began moving east.

    It had remained beyond the seawall’s engagement boundary throughout the incident.

    Latch pursued it along the surviving platforms until command ordered withdrawal. The pilot reported that the raised structure seen by the surveillance aircraft had lowered into the water.

    The coastal array recorded no further contact.

    Rain intensity decreased at 04:16.

    POST-INCIDENT FINDINGS

    All thirty-eight civilian transports were accounted for outside the district. Confirmed losses include the A3 seawall, two pumping stations, four kilometers of roadway, and Pathfinder unit Mercy.

    Foundation cores recovered from the breach showed smooth internal cavities below the original seawall footings. Mineral deposits along their surfaces indicate prolonged exposure to seawater.

    The cavities do not appear in construction surveys.

    Their full extent remains undetermined.

    Vale’s timing analysis has been retained in the restricted appendix. Investigators found no evidence that deployment orders were intercepted. The correspondence between planned movements and structural impacts remains unexplained.

    Personnel assigned to adjacent sectors have reported intermittent knocking beneath their maintenance galleries.

    They have been instructed to log the sounds without approaching them.

    INVESTIGATOR’S NOTE

    A3 was designated a coastal breach. That classification assumes the enemy began outside the perimeter.

    We can no longer support that assumption.
